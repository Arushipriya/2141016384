Linux was created in 1991 by Linus Torvalds as a free, open-source operating system kernel. Inspired by the UNIX system, Linux quickly grew with the help of developers worldwide. It was combined with GNU software, which had been developed since the 1980s by the Free Software Foundation to promote user freedom and open collaboration.
The GNU/Linux philosophy emphasizes freedom to use, modify, and share software. It values community development, transparency, and avoiding vendor lock-in. Together, GNU tools and the Linux kernel form a powerful, flexible, and secure operating system.
